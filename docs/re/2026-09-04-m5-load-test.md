# M5 load test

Date: 2026-09-04. Code: the M5 load step commits on `main` after `d5e0c01`, with the fixes of the final review and of the live runs (`8eef29a`, `08a45d6`).

This note records the exit test of spec `2026-09-04-m5-load-design.md` section 1.2. The game was not run. The test client does the steps that game clients do.

## Set-up

- One PC: i7-14700K (28 threads), 62 GB RAM. Docker PostgreSQL, Valkey and NATS.
- Release builds of `edge-http`, `world-director`, `sim`, `rep-gateway` and `nw-testclient`, all on this PC. The bots and the servers share the CPU.
- `edge-http serve` on port 8443, `auth.login_fail_limit` raised to 50,000 by an environment override (every bot registers from one address).
- `world-director` with a local config: `cell_size_m = 1024`, 18 `start_positions` on the borders of eight `sim` stripes. A throwaway search of the world file chose them: flat land (slope at most 0.7) over each bot area.
- Eight `sim` processes with `tick_threads = 4`, each with its own `sim_id` and metrics port.
- `rep-gateway serve` with 12 `workers`, `limits.max_connections` 12,000 and `limits.handshakes_per_ip_per_second` 1,000 (environment overrides).
- `nw-testclient swarm --bots <n>` with a local config: the same 18 `starts_m`, 50 bots started per second (a ramp: 250 bots every 5 s), lanes ± 150 m, swing ± 20 m, 4 m/s, at least 4 crossings, bundle gap at most 250 ms, 99 % of own bundles. Each bot stays 240 s (steps 1,000 to 5,000) or 320 s (step 10,000), so every step holds all its bots together for at least 120 s.
- `mpstat 5` and `pidstat 5` beside each step.

## Faults found and fixed during the live runs

The spec counts a step that fails with idle CPU left as a fault. Three such faults came up before the run above.

1. The committed `limits.max_connections` (2,000) refused every bot above 2,000. The README load test now says to raise it. The swarm now prints how many bots stopped with each error (`8eef29a`).
2. A gateway worker that fell behind lost world events from its full queue, also reliable login messages: a bot that lost its login message never saw itself. This happened only in the last seconds of the login rush, with 2 to 4 % idle CPU. Now a worker loses only bundles; reliable messages and closes always reach it, in order. The connection and handshake gauges carry a `worker` label, so the workers no longer overwrite one another (`08a45d6`).
3. At 100 bots per second, `edge-http` used about 5.5 cores for the password hashes. The live test now starts 50 bots per second.

## What this proves

- On one PC that also runs every bot, the M5 world carries 5,000 players spread over eight `sim` processes. Every player crosses a `sim` border at least 20 times; every crossing is a handoff within one tick; no player is closed or refused a move; every player gets at least 99.68 % of its bundles, with no gap above 93 ms.
- The 10,000 step fails at the limit of this PC, not at a fault found so far: the PC had at most 6 % idle CPU while the bots failed.

## What this does not prove

- 10,000 players on one PC. Unknown. The bots and the login rush, which a real server does not carry, used about 9.7 of the 28 threads at the peak. A run with the bots on a second machine would show the limit of the servers alone.
- The game itself in the world (blocked by the game's certificate trust, which this project does not change).
- Several gateway processes, or players spread over the whole map.
