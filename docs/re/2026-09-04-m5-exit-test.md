# M5 exit test

Date: 2026-09-04. Code: the M5 commits on `main` after `99d8acd`, with the fixes of the final review.

This note records the M5 exit test of spec `2026-09-04-m5-world-director-design.md` section 1.2. The game was not run. The test client does the steps that game clients do. The game cannot finish DTLS with this gateway (M3 spec section 1.3).

## Set-up

- One PC (28 threads, 62 GB RAM). Docker PostgreSQL, Valkey and NATS.
- Release builds of `edge-http`, `world-director`, `sim`, `rep-gateway` and `nw-testclient`.
- `edge-http migrate` (world migration 0003), then `edge-http serve` on port 8443 with the operator's own domain and certificate. `auth.login_fail_limit` raised by an environment override, because the edge counts every registration per IP address and all bots come from one address.
- `world-director` with a local copy of `config/world-director.toml`: `start_position` at x 2048, y 8600, on the border between the stripes of `sim-1` and `sim-2`. A throwaway search of the world file chose the point: land over the whole bot area (x 2048 ± 25 m, y 8600 ± 160 m), steepest slope 0.3.
- Four `sim` processes with the committed `config/sim.toml` and the world file of client build 22469132 (`newworld_vitaeeterna`), each with its own `sim_id` and metrics port. The director gave them the column stripes x 0 to 2048, 2048 to 6144, 6144 to 10240 and 10240 to 14336.
- `rep-gateway serve` with the committed `config/rep-gateway.toml` and `limits.handshakes_per_ip_per_second` raised to 200.
- `nw-testclient` with a local copy of `config/nw-testclient.toml`: the domain URLs with port 8443, and the committed `[swarm]` values (1,000 bots, 50 per second, lanes ± 150 m, swing ± 20 m, 4 m/s, 120 s, at least 4 crossings, bundle gap at most 250 ms), with `start_m` at the director's start and the metrics URLs of the four sims.
- The secrets are in a git-ignored local file.

## Result

|---|---|
| 1. `cargo test --workspace` and `just test-infra` pass | Pass. 624 and 207 tests, 0 failed. |

| 2.2 `nw-testclient swarm`, 1,000 bots | Pass. All ten checks. |

**Observed.** The two busy sims (`sim-1` and `sim-2`) took every tick in at most 20 ms; about 30 % of their ticks during the swarm took 10 to 20 ms. Every handoff took at most 20 ms. No program logged a warning or an error during the run.

**Observed.** This was the second swarm run with the same bot accounts. The bots appeared at their saved positions and walked from there to their lanes. The first run, before the review fixes, also passed every check of that time (10,431 handoffs, 0 failed).

## What this proves

- Criterion 2 of section 1.2, between programs that this project wrote, on the real world file: 1,000 players move back and forth across a `sim` border on the real ground. Every crossing is a handoff in at most 20 ms, below one tick. No player is refused a move, no player is closed, and no player misses its bundles for more than about 0.1 s. Every tick of the busy sims stays below one tick.
- The M4 checks hold through the director: login order, seeing each other, the walk, the refused far move, and the saved position.

## What this does not prove

- The game itself in the world. That is blocked by the game's built-in certificate trust, and this project does not change it.
- How the game shows a handoff, a ghost, or the jump back of a resumed player (Unknown).
- More than one gateway, more than 1,000 players, or players spread over the whole map. The busy sims used up to 20 ms per tick at 1,000 bots near one border, so the 10,000-player step needs its own load test.
- A `sim` that stops during a live run. The automated test `a_player_of_a_stopped_sim_continues_on_the_other` covers it on two sims; the live run did not stop a sim.
